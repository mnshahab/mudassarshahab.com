Resilience Economics Model

A structured model for evaluating the financial tradeoffs of resilience, redundancy, recovery objectives, service criticality, and business risk.

Step 1

Define Business Criticality

Start with the business impact of service interruption. Classify the workload by revenue impact, customer impact, operational dependency, regulatory exposure, reputational impact, and recovery priority.

Step 2

Define Recovery Requirements

Requirement Decision Question
Recovery Time Objective How quickly must the service be restored after disruption?
Recovery Point Objective How much data loss is acceptable?
Availability Target What level of service availability does the business require?
Failure Scope Must the architecture tolerate resource, zone, region, provider, or dependency failure?
Recovery Validation How often must failover, restore, and recovery procedures be tested?
Step 3

Compare Resilience Patterns

Pattern Economic Considerations
Backup and Restore Lowest steady-state cost, longer recovery time, restore testing, storage cost, and operational recovery effort.
Pilot Light Minimal standby footprint with higher recovery automation and activation requirements.
Warm Standby Higher steady-state cost in exchange for faster recovery and reduced activation effort.
Active-Passive Duplicate capacity, replication, network, data synchronization, and failover orchestration costs.
Active-Active Highest operating complexity and cost, with potential gains in availability, traffic distribution, and recovery speed.
Step 4

Calculate the Full Resilience Cost

Cost Component What to Include
Compute Standby, duplicate, reserved, burst, and failover capacity.
Storage Replication, snapshots, backups, archival copies, and retention.
Data Transfer Cross-zone, cross-region, replication, synchronization, and egress charges.
Database Replicas, clusters, standby nodes, replication, backup, and recovery services.
Network Load balancing, gateways, private connectivity, DNS, and failover routing.
Observability Monitoring, logging, tracing, alerting, synthetic testing, and recovery validation.
Operational Effort Engineering, testing, automation, drills, incident response, maintenance, and governance.
Step 5

Compare Cost to Business Exposure

Resilience should be evaluated against the financial exposure of downtime rather than treated as a technical requirement in isolation.

Compare the annual cost of the resilience pattern with expected outage exposure, lost revenue, productivity loss, regulatory impact, recovery cost, customer impact, and contractual penalties.

Step 6

Document the Resilience Decision

Decision Field Required Evidence
Business Criticality Documented impact classification and recovery priority.
Target RTO and RPO Approved recovery time and recovery point objectives.
Selected Pattern Chosen resilience architecture and failure scope.
Annual Resilience Cost Expected steady-state and operational cost of the selected pattern.
Business Exposure Estimated financial and operational impact of service disruption.
Key Assumptions Demand, recovery behavior, failure frequency, capacity, replication, and pricing assumptions.
Review Trigger Changes in criticality, architecture, regulation, cost, demand, or recovery requirements.
