30-60-90 Day Implementation Plan

A practical sequence for applying the operating model from The Economically Architected Cloud within an existing cloud environment.

Days 1-30

Establish Visibility and Ownership

The first 30 days should establish a reliable baseline. Focus on ownership, cloud cost data, financial visibility, material risks, and decision accountability.

Workstream Primary Actions Expected Output
Cloud Inventory Document accounts, subscriptions, projects, major platforms, workloads, regions, and shared services. Cloud estate baseline.
Ownership Map business, engineering, financial, and platform owners to material cloud spend. Ownership register.
Cost Data Validate billing ingestion, cost treatment, allocation coverage, and invoice reconciliation. Trusted cost baseline.
Architecture Review Identify material cost drivers, resilience patterns, network dependencies, data gravity, and platform concentration. Architecture economics findings.
Commercial Position Document commitments, enterprise discounts, private pricing, credits, support plans, and contract obligations. Commercial baseline.
Risk Identification Identify unallocated spend, commitment exposure, high-cost workloads, resilience overdesign, and structural inefficiencies. Prioritized risk register.
Days 31-60

Prioritize and Correct Economic Gaps

The second phase should turn the baseline into decisions. Prioritize the highest-value economic, architectural, commercial, and governance corrections.

Workstream Primary Actions Expected Output
Workload Placement Review workloads with weak technical, economic, or strategic placement. Placement decision backlog.
Optimization Prioritize rightsizing, idle-resource removal, scheduling, storage optimization, network efficiency, and service modernization. Optimization roadmap.
Allocation Correct ownership gaps, shared-cost allocation logic, metadata quality, and unmapped spend. Improved allocation coverage.
Forecasting Build demand-based forecasts tied to workload changes, growth assumptions, migrations, and commitments. Forecast baseline.
Commercial Strategy Review commitment coverage, discount structures, renewal timing, portability, and underutilization risk. Commercial action plan.
Governance Define decision rights, approval thresholds, exception handling, and review cadence. Governance operating model.
Days 61-90

Operationalize the Model

The final phase should move from project activity into repeatable operating discipline. Establish recurring reviews, measurable outcomes, ownership, and decision evidence.

Workstream Primary Actions Expected Output
Operating Cadence Establish weekly, monthly, and quarterly reviews for cost, forecast, optimization, commitments, and architecture decisions. Recurring FinOps cadence.
KPI Framework Define metrics for allocation, forecast accuracy, optimization execution, commitment performance, and unit economics. Management scorecard.
Engineering Accountability Assign remediation owners, due dates, economic impact, risk acceptance, and escalation paths. Execution accountability model.
Architecture Economics Embed cost, resilience, placement, network, and commercial analysis into architecture reviews. Economic architecture gate.
Executive Reporting Report material trends, risks, savings realization, forecast variance, and business-value indicators. Executive operating view.
Continuous Improvement Define review triggers for material changes in demand, architecture, pricing, contracts, regulation, and business strategy. Ongoing improvement cycle.

90-Day Outcome

By day 90, the organization should have a trusted cloud cost baseline, clear ownership, prioritized economic actions, defined decision rights, a working operating cadence, and a roadmap for deeper architectural and financial improvement.

The objective is not to complete every optimization within 90 days. The objective is to establish a repeatable system for making, executing, and reviewing cloud economic decisions.
