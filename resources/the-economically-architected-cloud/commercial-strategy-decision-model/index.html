Commercial Strategy Decision Model

A structured model for evaluating cloud pricing, discounts, commitments, contracts, incentives, support economics, and long-term commercial tradeoffs.

Step 1

Establish the Demand Baseline

Start with expected workload demand before evaluating commercial terms. Document current spend, forecasted consumption, workload stability, growth expectations, architecture changes, migration plans, and known demand uncertainty.

Step 2

Separate Rate Optimization from Usage Optimization

Dimension Decision Focus
Usage Optimization Reduce unnecessary consumption through rightsizing, scheduling, modernization, storage management, and waste removal.
Rate Optimization Reduce the effective price of required consumption through discounts, commitments, negotiated pricing, and purchasing constructs.
Step 3

Evaluate Commercial Levers

Commercial Lever What to Evaluate
Enterprise Discounts Discount scope, eligibility, spend thresholds, term, exclusions, and interaction with other pricing constructs.
Commitment Programs Coverage, utilization risk, duration, portability, break-even point, flexibility, and workload stability.
Private Pricing Negotiated service rates, minimum spend, term length, scope, and expected effective savings.
Migration Incentives Credits, funding, professional services support, expiration terms, and dependency on migration milestones.
Support Plans Support fees, service levels, escalation requirements, and whether support pricing scales with total spend.
Marketplace Economics Marketplace commitments, vendor consolidation, private offers, software pricing, and impact on enterprise spend commitments.
Contract Flexibility Renewal terms, exit provisions, portability, renegotiation rights, minimum spend obligations, and termination exposure.
Step 4

Measure Effective Economics

Compare commercial options using effective cost rather than headline discount percentage.

Effective economics should include expected utilization, unused commitments, support charges, credits, incentives, minimum spend obligations, migration costs, contract duration, and workload uncertainty.

Step 5

Test Commitment Risk

Risk Area Decision Question
Demand Stability Is the committed usage expected to remain stable throughout the term?
Architecture Change Will modernization, migration, or service substitution reduce committed demand?
Business Change Could divestiture, acquisition, product retirement, or demand contraction change the consumption profile?
Portability Can the commitment move across services, regions, accounts, or eligible workloads?
Break-Even At what utilization level does the commercial construct outperform the flexible alternative?
Step 6

Document the Commercial Decision

Decision Field Required Evidence
Selected Commercial Model Discount structure, commitment model, pricing arrangement, or contract strategy.
Financial Rationale Expected effective savings, total cost impact, and break-even analysis.
Demand Assumptions Consumption forecast, workload stability, growth, migration, and modernization assumptions.
Commercial Dependencies Minimum spend, eligibility, term, credits, support structure, and contractual dependencies.
Risk Exposure Underutilization, lock-in, stranded commitment, renewal, portability, and contract risk.
Review Trigger Conditions requiring the commercial decision to be reviewed or renegotiated.
