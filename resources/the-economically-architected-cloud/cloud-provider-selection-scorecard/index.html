Cloud Provider Selection Scorecard

A two-stage decision model for evaluating cloud provider fit. Stage 1 removes providers that fail mandatory constraints. Stage 2 compares the remaining providers using weighted economic, technical, commercial, and operating criteria.

Stage 1

Mandatory Constraint Gates

A provider that fails a mandatory requirement should not advance into weighted scoring. These gates protect the decision from being distorted by attractive pricing or feature scores that do not meet fundamental workload requirements.

Constraint Decision Question Result
Data Residency Does the provider meet required geographic and regulatory data-location requirements? Pass / Fail
Service Capability Does the provider support the required services, architectures, and workload patterns? Pass / Fail
Security and Compliance Does the provider meet required security, privacy, compliance, and control requirements? Pass / Fail
Network Fit Does the provider meet connectivity, latency, routing, and integration requirements? Pass / Fail
Data Gravity Does existing data location make the provider operationally and economically viable? Pass / Fail
Licensing Constraints Are software licensing terms compatible with the proposed provider and deployment model? Pass / Fail
Commercial Eligibility Does the organization qualify for required contracts, incentives, commitments, or commercial programs? Pass / Fail
Stage 2

Weighted Provider Comparison

Only providers that pass Stage 1 should be scored here. Weight each criterion according to workload and business priorities. Scores should be supported by evidence rather than preference.

Criterion What to Evaluate Suggested Weight
Architecture Fit Service maturity, workload compatibility, performance, scalability, resilience, and platform integration. 15%
Rate Economics On-demand rates, negotiated pricing, enterprise discounts, reservations, savings constructs, and effective unit cost. 15%
Data and Transfer Economics Storage pricing, data movement, egress, inter-region transfer, replication, and network-related cost. 10%
Commitment Strategy Commitment flexibility, coverage options, utilization risk, portability, duration, and break-even economics. 10%
Commercial Incentives Credits, migration funding, enterprise agreements, private pricing, support concessions, and incentive durability. 10%
Operational Fit Existing skills, tooling, automation, governance, support model, operating maturity, and engineering productivity. 10%
Security and Governance Identity, policy enforcement, auditability, compliance integration, control maturity, and governance overhead. 10%
Support Economics Support plan pricing, service expectations, escalation requirements, and operational dependency on provider support. 5%
Sustainability Environmental reporting, energy efficiency, regional sustainability considerations, and organizational requirements. 5%
Multi-Cloud Operating Burden Additional people, tooling, governance, security, networking, data, and support costs introduced by another provider. 10%

Scoring Method

Score each provider from 1 to 5 for every weighted criterion. Multiply the score by the assigned weight. Add the weighted values to produce the provider's total score.

A higher score does not automatically make a provider the right choice. The final decision should also document major assumptions, financial dependencies, migration costs, concentration risk, and conditions that might change the recommendation.
